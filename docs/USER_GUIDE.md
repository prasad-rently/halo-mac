# Halo — User Guide

**Your Mac. Elevated.**

Welcome to Halo! This guide walks you through everything Halo can do, in plain
language. You don't need to be technical to follow along — each section explains
what a feature is, why it's useful, and exactly how to use it.

> **The golden rule:** Halo never deletes anything without asking you first, and
> when it does remove something, it goes to the **Trash** — so you can always get
> it back. Nothing is ever erased for good behind your back.

<a name="getting-started"></a>
## 1. Getting started

When you open Halo for the first time, a short **welcome walkthrough** helps you
get set up. You'll be asked a few simple questions — like how often you'd like
Halo to check your Mac, and how you'd like it to appear in your menu bar. You can
change any of these later in **Settings**, so don't worry about getting it
"right" the first time.

Halo's window has a **sidebar** down the left. Each item in the sidebar is a
different tool (Dashboard, Cleanup, Protection, and so on). Click any item to open
that tool. **Dashboard** stays at the top; you can **drag the other items** up or
down to put your favourites first — click the little slider button at the top of
the sidebar to rearrange them, then click it again when you're done.

<a name="dashboard"></a>
## 2. The Dashboard — your Mac at a glance

**What it is:** Your home base. The Dashboard shows a big **health score** from 0
to 100 that sums up how well your Mac is doing right now.

**What you'll see:**
- A **health ring** with your score. Higher is better. If it dips, Halo will
  suggest what to look at.
- Live cards for **CPU** (how hard the processor is working), **Memory** (RAM),
  **Disk** (storage), **Network** (internet speed), and **Battery**.
- A **Smart Scan** button to check everything at once.
- An **Export Report** button to save a tidy PDF summary of your Mac's health.
- An **Alert History** section showing recent notifications from Halo.

**How to use it:** Just glance at it. If the score is green, you're in great shape.
If something needs attention, tap **Smart Scan** and Halo will point you to the
right tool.

<a name="cleanup"></a>
## 3. Cleanup — free up space

**What it is:** A safe way to clear out the junk files your Mac collects over
time — temporary files, caches, old logs, and leftovers from apps like Xcode.

**How to use it:**
1. Open **Cleanup**. Halo automatically scans and sorts what it finds into
   categories (like *System Caches*, *Logs*, *Trash*).
2. Pick a category to see exactly what's inside.
3. Click **Clean All** when you're ready.
4. Halo shows you a **confirmation** — *"Move these items to Trash?"* — before it
   touches anything. Click to confirm, or cancel if you change your mind.

**Is it safe?** Yes. Cleanup only targets throwaway files, always asks first, and
everything goes to the Trash (not deleted forever), so you can restore anything.

<a name="protection"></a>
## 4. Protection — stay safe from bad software

**What it is:** A scanner that checks your Mac for **malware, adware, and other
unwanted software** using an up-to-date list of known threats.

**How to use it:**
1. Open **Protection** and click **Run Full Scan**.
2. Halo checks the places bad software likes to hide and shows you the results.
3. If it finds nothing, you'll see a reassuring "all clear". If it finds
   something, you can choose to remove it — and Halo will **ask you to confirm**
   first.

**Good to know:** Halo keeps its threat list current automatically, so scans stay
effective over time.

<a name="performance"></a>
## 5. Performance — keep things running smoothly

**What it is:** A set of tools to see what's using your Mac's resources and to do
light maintenance.

**What's inside:**
- **Top Processes** — a live list of the apps using the most CPU or memory. Tap
  the picker to sort by CPU or by memory.
- **Battery** — your battery's charge, whether it's charging, and its overall
  health (Excellent / Good / Fair, etc.). On desktop Macs it simply says there's
  no battery.
- **Network** — your live download/upload activity and whether you're on a VPN.
- **Speed Test** — check your real internet download/upload speed with one click.
- **Login Items** — the apps that start automatically when you log in. You can
  review them, and jump to the system settings to manage them.
- **Idle Apps** — apps you've left open but haven't used in a while. Halo can
  offer to quit them to free up memory — and it always **asks first** before
  quitting anything.

**How to use it:** Open **Performance** and browse the sections. It's mostly for
looking; any action that closes an app or changes a setting asks for your OK.

<a name="applications"></a>
## 6. Applications — remove apps completely

**What it is:** When you drag an app to the Trash the normal way, it often leaves
behind hidden support files scattered around your Mac. Halo's uninstaller finds
**all** of those leftovers and removes them together, so nothing is left behind.

**How to use it:**
1. Open **Applications**. Halo lists everything installed.
2. Click an app to see its details, including the **leftover files** it would
   remove (preferences, caches, logs, and so on).
3. Click **Uninstall**.
4. Halo shows a **confirmation** listing what will be removed. Confirm to move it
   all to the Trash, or cancel.

**Is it safe?** Yes — it always confirms first, and everything goes to the Trash.

<a name="files"></a>
## 7. Files — find what's eating your storage

The **Files** tool has several tabs along the top:

- **SpaceLens** — a visual map of what's taking up space, so you can see at a
  glance which folders are the biggest.
- **Duplicates** — finds files that are exact copies of each other (great for
  reclaiming space). Click **Scan Home** to check your common folders, or
  **Choose Folder** to scan a specific place. Halo automatically marks the older
  copies for removal and keeps the newest; you can **click any copy to change
  what's marked**. When you click **Delete marked**, Halo **confirms first**, then
  moves the extras to the Trash.
- **Downloads** — helps you tidy your Downloads folder. You can remove a single
  file, or use **Clean Stale** to clear out old downloads. Either way, Halo
  **asks before deleting**.
- **Large Files** — lists your biggest files (over 500 MB) so you can decide what
  to let go. Removing one **asks for confirmation** first.
- **Drive Speed** — measures how fast your internal disk (or an external drive)
  can read and write. Pick a test size and click start; Halo reports both the
  average and peak speeds. This test is completely safe — it only writes a
  temporary file that it removes right after.

**Is it safe?** Every delete in Files asks first and uses the Trash.

<a name="clipboard"></a>
## 8. Clipboard — never lose something you copied

**What it is:** Your Mac normally only remembers the *last* thing you copied.
Halo remembers a whole **history** — text, links, images, colours, and code
snippets — so you can paste something you copied earlier.

**How to use it:**
- Open **Clipboard** to browse everything you've copied recently. You can filter
  by type and search.
- Press **⌘⇧V** (Command-Shift-V) anywhere to pop up a quick picker, choose an
  item, and paste it instantly.
- To wipe your history, use **Clear History** — Halo will confirm first.

**Your privacy:** Your clipboard history stays on your Mac.

<a name="snippets"></a>
## 9. Snippets — save text you type often

**What it is:** Bits of text you reuse a lot — your address, a standard reply, a
signature, a code template — saved so you can drop them in anytime.

**How to use it:** In the **Clipboard** area, open the **Snippets** section, click
to add a new snippet, give it a name, and paste in the text. From then on it's one
click (or search) away whenever you need it.

<a name="actions"></a>
## 10. Actions — one-click shortcuts for common tasks

**What it is:** A library of over a hundred handy one-click tasks — things that
would normally need the Terminal or digging through settings. Examples:
- Flush the DNS cache, free up memory, empty the Trash
- Clear caches for developer tools, creative apps, and browsers
- Generate a secure password or a unique ID
- Toggle Dark Mode, take a screenshot, mute the mic
- Format or clean up whatever's on your clipboard

**How to use it:**
1. Open **Actions**. Browse by category or **search** for what you want (Halo's
   search is forgiving — partial words work).
2. Click an action to run it. Simple, safe actions run right away.
3. Some actions need your macOS password (they change system settings) or ask you
   to confirm — Halo will always prompt before doing anything with real
   consequences.
4. Press **⌘⇧A** (Command-Shift-A) anywhere to open the quick action picker
   without switching to Halo.

**Make your own:** Click **New Action** to create a custom shortcut with your own
name, icon, and command.

<a name="ports"></a>
## 11. Ports — see what's using your network connections

**What it is:** A "port" is a numbered doorway your Mac uses for network
connections. This tool is mostly for developers: it shows which apps are
listening on which ports — handy when something is "already using port 3000".

**How to use it:**
1. Open **Ports** to see the list. Use the search box to find a specific port or
   app.
2. To stop a process, click the **Kill** button on its row. Halo **asks you to
   confirm** ("Kill this process?") before doing anything — so you never end an
   app by accident.
3. You can give a port a friendly name so you remember what it's for.

<a name="code-beautifier"></a>
## 12. Code Beautifier — tidy up code and text

**What it is:** Paste in messy code or data (like a cramped one-line JSON blob)
and Halo formats it neatly with proper indentation and colour highlighting — easy
to read and copy back out.

**How to use it:** Open the beautifier, paste your text, pick the language if
needed, and click to format. Nothing on your Mac is changed — it just works on the
text you paste in.

<a name="ai-assistant"></a>
## 13. AI Assistant — ask questions about your Mac

**What it is:** A chat assistant, built into Halo, that can answer questions and
even check your Mac's live stats for you — for example, *"What's my health score
and what's using the most memory?"* It can use Claude, OpenAI, or Google Gemini.

**How to set it up:** The AI Assistant uses **your own account** with one of those
providers, so you'll paste in an **API key** from the provider you choose. Your key
is stored securely in your Mac's **Keychain**, is never shown again, and is only
ever sent to that provider — never to Halo. If you haven't added a key yet, the
assistant simply shows a "connect your key" screen and does nothing else.

**How to use it:**
1. Open **AI Assistant**, choose a provider, and paste your key once.
2. Type your question and press send.
3. The assistant can read safe, read-only information (like your CPU or battery)
   on its own. If it wants to *do* something — like run a Smart Scan or export a
   report — it **asks your permission first**.
4. Prefer a quick question without leaving what you're doing? Press **⌘⇧I**
   (Command-Shift-I) for a floating quick-ask box.

<a name="menu-bar"></a>
## 14. The Menu Bar — Halo at the top of your screen

**What it is:** A small Halo indicator that lives in your Mac's menu bar (top-right
of the screen) so you can see your Mac's status without opening the app.

**Choose how it looks:** Open the **Menu Bar** settings and pick a style:
- **Icon** — just the Halo icon (tidy and minimal)
- **Text stats** — live numbers like `CPU 42% · RAM 61%`
- **Mini bars** — tiny progress bars for CPU and memory
- **Dot** — a single coloured dot (green = good, amber = busy, red = under
  pressure)
- **Custom** — build your own readout by mixing in the values you care about
  (CPU, RAM, disk, battery, network, temperature, and more). There are ready-made
  presets to start from, and a live preview as you edit.

---

<a name="widget"></a>
## 15. The Widget — Halo in Notification Center

**What it is:** A Halo widget you can add to your **Notification Center** (or
desktop) to see your Mac's health and key stats at a glance.

**How to add it:** Open Notification Center, scroll down and click **Edit
Widgets**, find **Halo**, and choose a size — **Small**, **Medium**, or **Large**.
The widget refreshes about once a minute on its own.

---

<a name="smart-scan"></a>
## 16. Smart Scan & scheduling — set it and forget it

**What it is:** **Smart Scan** checks the important things — cleanup
opportunities, health, and more — in one pass. Importantly, it only *looks*; it
never deletes anything on its own. Anything it suggests removing still goes
through the normal confirm-first process.

**Run it anytime:** Click **Smart Scan** on the Dashboard.

**Automate it:** In **Settings**, set a schedule — say, every Monday at 3 AM — and
Halo will quietly run a scan in the background and let you know if it finds
anything worth your attention. You choose the day and time.

<a name="alerts-reports"></a>
## 17. Alerts & reports — stay informed

- **Alerts:** When something needs your attention, Halo sends a notification and
  keeps a running **Alert History** (the last 50) on the Dashboard, so you can
  catch up on anything you missed. Unread alerts show a badge; you can mark them
  read or clear them.
- **PDF Report:** Click **Export Report** on the Dashboard to save a clean,
  four-page PDF summary of your Mac's health — great for keeping a record or
  sharing with support. You choose where to save it.

<a name="siri"></a>
## 18. Siri & Shortcuts — hands-free Halo

Halo plugs into Apple's **Shortcuts** app and **Siri**, so you can ask for things
by voice or build them into your own automations. For example:
- "Hey Siri, what's my Mac's health score?"
- Get your CPU usage, battery health, or free disk space
- Run a Smart Scan or a specific Action
- Grab your recent clipboard items
- Export a health report

Open the **Shortcuts** app and search for **Halo** to see everything available and
add it to your routines.

<a name="faq"></a>
## 20. Frequently asked questions

**Will Halo ever delete something without asking?**
No. Every removal shows a confirmation first, and everything goes to the **Trash**,
so you can restore it.

**Is my data private?**
Yes. Your clipboard history, snippets, and settings stay on your Mac. The AI
Assistant only works if *you* add your own provider key, and that key is stored
securely and only sent to the provider you chose.

**Does Halo send analytics?**
Only if you opt in. It's off by default, and even when on, it never includes
personal information.

**Something looks off — how do I get a summary to share?**
Use **Export Report** on the Dashboard to create a PDF of your Mac's current
health.

**Can I change where things are and how Halo looks?**
Yes — rearrange the sidebar by dragging, choose your menu-bar style, set your scan
schedule, and more in **Settings**.
